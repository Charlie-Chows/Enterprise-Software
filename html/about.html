ORIGIN

A practice,
not a product.

FIG. A — NARRATIVE INDEX

  1. 01

    The problem was never the software.

    Every operations team we met had capable tools. What they lacked was a shared model of how those tools related to each other. Diagrams were out of date, permissions were implicit, and every new hire learned the system by asking the person beside them.

  2. 02

    So we started with the diagram.

    The first thing we built was not a service — it was a spec. A single document that described services, contracts, ownership, and the rules that governed access between them. It was versioned, reviewed, and diffed like any other engineering artifact.

  3. 03

    The platform grew out of it.

    Once the spec was authoritative, the platform's job became clear: keep it honest. Modules read the spec. Policies resolve against it. Environments are provisioned from it. Nothing drifts without being visible in a diff.

  4. 04

    The rest is discipline.

    Shipping platform software for critical operations is not a creativity problem. It is a restraint problem — knowing what to leave out, when to make something explicit, and when a simpler primitive will outlast a clever abstraction.

PRINCIPLES OF PRACTICE

Five rules we do not negotiate.

Each principle is written as a rule because it must survive disagreement. Expand to read the reasoning behind it.

P-01 Explicit over implicit

If a system depends on something — a credential, a network path, an assumed data shape — that dependency is written down and versioned. Implicit knowledge is a failure mode disguised as efficiency. The cost of writing it down is a few minutes; the cost of not writing it down is measured in hours of on-call time.

P-02 Reversible over clever

Any change we ship comes with a rehearsed path back. That path is tested in the same environment where the change will run — not in a laptop approximation. Clever solutions that cannot be undone are not solutions; they are commitments that outlive the problem.

P-03 Measured over assumed

If a claim cannot be measured on the platform itself, it does not belong on a slide. We publish the measurement method alongside the result. Anyone reading our numbers should be able to reproduce them without asking us how.

P-04 Readable by the next operator

Every artifact — spec, policy, runbook — is written for the person who will read it in two years, at 3am, with no context. If a document needs a call to understand, the document is wrong, not the reader.

P-05 Own the whole diagram

Partial ownership creates partial accountability. If we take on a domain, we take on its edges, its dependencies, and its failure modes. We do not accept boundaries that exist only to make the diagram prettier.

Principles describe our practice. They are not a contractual commitment.

GOVERNANCE POSTURE

How control is held.

Four dimensions of governance, each with a defined owner and an auditable trace. Select to view the posture for each.

DIM-01 · ACCESS

Every decision is declarative.

Access is resolved from a versioned policy set. There are no backdoor grants, no shared service accounts, and no implicit inheritance from directory group names. Every resolution is logged with the rule that fired.

  • OWNER Platform Security
  • REVIEW Quarterly, plus on role change
  • TRACE Per-decision, exportable

DIM-02 · CHANGE

Change is a typed event.

Every modification to the platform — a policy, an environment, a contract — enters through the same change process. No direct edits. The record of a change is durable, searchable, and survives the person who made it.

  • OWNER Platform Engineering
  • REVIEW Pre-merge, two reviewers
  • TRACE Immutable change log

DIM-03 · DATA

Data carries its own lineage.

Every record that enters the platform is stamped with its source, its contract version, and the policy under which it was accepted. Storage boundaries are declared, not inferred. Exports inherit the same access rules as the interface.

  • OWNER Data Platform
  • REVIEW On contract revision
  • TRACE Full lineage, per record

DIM-04 · INCIDENT

Incidents produce artifacts.

An incident is not closed when the service is back up. It is closed when the model has been updated, the runbook reflects the change, and the review has been written down where the next operator can find it.

  • OWNER Reliability
  • REVIEW Within five working days
  • TRACE Published review, indexed
